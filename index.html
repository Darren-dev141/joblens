Inventing Situations / research instrument 001

Before you apply,
verify the evidence.

In one breath: JobLens checks whether a job posting is still real. It follows each listing back to the employer’s own website and writes down what it finds — a working apply link, an expired post, or an honest “couldn’t verify.” What began as a 10-posting hand audit on October 22, 2026 is now a running pipeline: 1,510 postings screened and classified, a live feed re-verified against employer sources every run, and an application ledger that marks its own unconfirmed entries as unconfirmed. Missing evidence is never dressed up as proof of a fake job.

What this dataset actually shows

The verification rule

Employer apply evidence is stronger than a third-party advertisement. An explicit expired banner describes that platform's state, not necessarily the employer's. A missing official requisition is stronger evidence of stale distribution, not of intentional deception. Two similar titles without matching requisition IDs are possible duplicates, never automatically counted as one. All findings retain URLs, dates and observer roles.

The same rule runs the application ledger: of 232 recorded applications (snapshot October 25, 2026), 187 carry a submission confirmation — 158 confirmed, and 29 confirmed and later rejected by the employer. The 27 the ledger itself marks unconfirmed are reported as unconfirmed, never rounded up.

This page is the public snapshot of a working job-search instrument — evidence modeling you can inspect, not a statistical study of all listings.
