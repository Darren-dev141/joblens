Before you apply,
verify the evidence.

In one breath: JobLens checks whether a job posting is still real. It follows each listing back to the employer’s own website and writes down what it finds — a working apply link, an expired post, or an honest “couldn’t verify.” In this 10-posting pilot, only 2 had a confirmed employer apply link, 1 was already expired, and 4 could not be verified either way. Missing evidence is never dressed up as proof of a fake job.

What this dataset actually shows

These are the first ten results of one ranked HiringCafe Baltimore-area analyst page, not a representative census. One independent reviewer rechecked five selected rows. The evidence captures only what was observable on October 22, 2026. We cannot estimate a platform-wide error rate, employer intent to hire, or whether any listing is currently active from this snapshot.

The verification rule

Employer apply evidence is stronger than a third-party advertisement. An explicit expired banner describes that platform's state, not necessarily the employer's. A missing official requisition is stronger evidence of stale distribution, not of intentional deception. Two similar titles without matching requisition IDs are possible duplicates, never automatically counted as one. All findings retain URLs, dates and observer roles.

This is a portfolio demonstration of evidence modeling and reproducible auditing, not a live job-search service or a statistical study of all listings.
